# OVERVIEW

Towns on the Old West frontier are full of danger and adventure around every corner! The Frontier Town Expansion allows players a deeper and richer Town experience between adventures, adding exciting new types of Towns to find (such as Plague Towns, Outlaw Towns, or Mutant Towns), 6 all new Town Locations to visit (such as the Street Market, Sheriff’s Office, Indian Trading Post, or Smuggler’s Den), a new expanded Travel Chart, and a deadly new ranged Enemy
– Bandits, for use both down in the Mines as well as in Town!

With this expansion, Heroes also have the opportunity to play out Adventures in the Frontier Town itself using the new Town Adventure Board! Heroes can protect the townsfolk while battling demons in the streets, have a shootout with Bandits trying to rob the local bank, or find themselves in a duel at high noon! With loads of new charts, cards, counters, Missions, and the new Bandit Enemies, you’ll never know what to expect when you ride into a Frontier Town in Shadows of Brimstone!

### New Town Maps
There are 3 new Town Maps included, giving Heroes the option of traveling to a Small, Medium, or Large size Town. These new Town Maps do not have the specific Building Locations printed on them, but rather have a number of empty Location Slots that are filled with Random Town Location Building Tokens each time the Heroes arrive in a new Town.

### Town Adventure Board
A large, folding Town Adventure Board has also been included, allowing Heroes to play Missions within the Town itself!

### New Cards
Also included are a new deck of Daily Event cards for use in Town, some new Gear cards, Threat cards for the new Bandit Enemies, and a set of Infamous Bandit Gang cards for encountering tougher, well-known and wanted Bandits in the region.

### New Town Locations and Specialty Towns
There are 6 new large Town Location cards to represent the new Locations the Heroes may find in Town, as well as 8 double-sided Specialty Town Cards, each covering a different Type of Town the Heroes can find in their travels.

### New Enemies – Bandits
A common sight in any Town on the frontier is Bandits!  These rustlers and outlaws are a featured part of the Frontier Town Expansion, giving a new Enemy that can be encountered in the Mines, but also an Earthly foe that the Heroes can battle in Town during Missions on the Town Adventure Board.

### New Missions
There are 6 new Missions in the Frontier Town Expansion, all using the new Town Adventure Board.

### Unwanted Attention Markers
Sometimes Heroes will find that they have attracted the attention of an unsavory element in Town. This could be from bragging too many Marathons or from being a high-roller in the Saloon or Gambling Hall. These markers track a Hero’s level of Unwanted Attention.

### Town Location
Building Tokens
Each Town Location is now represented by a Building Token (the Core Set Town Locations as well as the new Town Locations). These Building Tokens are used to construct the Town.

### Shootout Markers
These markers are used for Enemies that focus on Ranged Attacks and have the Shootout Standardized Ability, such as Bandits.

### Townsfolk
These markers are used to represent Townsfolk during some of the Town Adventure Missions.

### New Side Bag Tokens
There are several new Side Bag Tokens available in Town for the Heroes to acquire, each with a new set of markers to represent them.

### The Devil’s Wheel
The Devil’s Wheel token is used for playing the Devil’s Wheel mini-game found at the Gambling Hall Town Location.

### Prisoner
This marker is used for the Town Adventure Jail Break Mission and is double-sided, with the Jailed Prisoner on the front and the Escaping Prisoner on the back.

### Money Bags
These markers are used for the Town Adventure Bank Robbery Mission.

### Gallows
This large marker is used for the Town Adventure Hanging High Mission.

### Additional Counters
Several additional counters have been provided. These are not needed for the main game but can be used for house rules and Missions, as well as future official content.

EXPANDED TOWN

The expanded Town material in this expansion greatly increases the variety your Heroes will find each time they finish an Adventure and Travel to Town. It is designed to have several different parts that all work together to build an exciting and different Town experience each time.

Small, Medium, and Large Towns

After an Adventure is complete and the Heroes are ready Travel to Town, the first thing they must now do is decide what Size of Town they would like to visit - Small Town (4 Locations), Medium Town (6 Locations), or Large Town (8 Locations).

Each Size of Town has a different Town Map with a number of open Town Location Slots on it. These Slots will be filled with Random Town Location Buildings once the Heroes arrive, meaning that you can never be certain of exactly which Locations will be available to you when you reach Town - the larger the Town, the more likely it will have a particular Location you may be hoping to visit.

The Size of Town the Heroes decide to Travel to also determines how many Travel Hazards the Heroes may face along the way.

## Expanded Travel Chart

A new Expanded Travel Chart is included at the back of this rulebook, more than doubling the possible Hazards and Events that the Heroes can encounter when traveling to Town.  This new chart uses a D36 (rolling two dice, with one as the ‘10’s’ digit and one as the ‘1’s’ digit) rather than the previous 3D6 roll.

The number of Travel Hazards that the Heroes will encounter has also changed, and is now based on the size of Town the Heroes are trying to reach.

##### Small Town
Each Hero adds a Travel Hazard on the D6 roll of 1, 2, or 3.

##### Medium Town
Each Hero automatically adds a Travel Hazard.

##### Large Town
Each Hero automatically adds a Travel Hazard, and rolls a D6.  On the roll of 1, 2, or 3, the Hero adds an additional Travel Hazard.

**Specialty Town Types**

Once the Heroes have completed their Traveling and have reached Town, they should roll to see what Type of Town they have found. On average, the Heroes will find a normal Frontier Town without any special rules associated. On occasion though, the Heroes will instead find that the Town they have reached is a Specialty Type of Town, using one of the large Specialty Town cards.

### Constructing the Town

To construct the Town that the Heroes have arrived at, place the appropriate Size Town Map (Small, Medium, or Large) on the table where all of the players can reach it. Then shuffle up all of the Town Location Building Tokens and place one Random token, face up in each of the empty Building Slots on the Town Map. These are the Locations that may be visited during the Heroes’ Town Stay (in addition to the Hotel and Camp Site, which are printed on the Map).

Note that several of the Specialty Town Types will ‘always’ or ‘never’ include certain Locations in a Town of that Type. When this is the case, those Location Tokens that are always present should be placed first, and then any that are never present should be removed before filling the rest of the Building Slots with Random Locations.

### Destroyed Building Slots

When a Building is Destroyed during the Town Stay, simply flip that Building Token over in its Slot (with the plain, black side up) to show that it has been Destroyed and may no longer be visited.

When one or more Buildings is Destroyed from the beginning of the Town Stay (due to failing a Mission, a Travel Hazard, etc), Construct the Town as normal, and then roll for which Random Building Slots are Destroyed, flipping those Building Tokens facedown in the Slot as above.

### New Town Locations

There are six new Town Locations available as part of the Frontier Town Expansion, including - the Sheriff’s Office, Smuggler’s Den, Indian Trading Post, Mutant Quarter, Gambling Hall, and Street Market. Each of these Locations has a large reference card showing the Location Event Chart as well as all of the things that a Hero can do and buy when visiting that Location.

It is important to note that a couple of these Locations (Sheriff’s Office and Smuggler’s Den) have restrictions on who can and cannot visit them. These restrictions are listed on the Location’s reference card, just above the Location Event Chart.

### Visiting the Camp Site as a Location

One additional option now available while in Town is that Heroes may spend their Day visiting the Camp Site as a Town Location. One of the large reference cards has the Camp Site Location on it, including the Location Event Chart and the things that a Hero may do and buy while there. The main benefit of visiting the Camp Site is that it has a Doc’s Tent and a Church Tent which can be used to attempt to Heal Injuries, Mutations, Madness, etc., even if the Doc’s Office or Church Buildings are not available or have been Destroyed.

Note that there is no Building Token for the Camp Site as it is always included as part of the Town Map. Also note that the Camp Site is not a numbered Building Slot, and so will never be Destroyed as a ‘Random Building’ (it can still be Destroyed by any effect that specifically targets the Camp Site however!).

## DAILY EVENT DECK

The Daily Event Deck adds a new element to your Town Stay, as minor events that crop up at the start of each Day in Town. When constructing the Town, shuffle the Daily Event Deck and place it near the Town Map.

At the start of each Day in Town (before Heroes decide to stay at the Hotel or Camp Site), draw one Daily Event card and read it aloud to all players. This card applies to all Heroes in Town and will either have an immediate effect or a continuing effect during the current Day. At the end of the current Day in Town, discard this Daily Event.

### Just Another Day on the Frontier

One common Daily Event card that has several copies in the deck is Just Another Day on the Frontier. This card indicates that it is just a normal day on the frontier and has no positive or negative effect on the current Day in Town. However, while in a Specialty Town Type, there are no ‘normal’ Days. As noted on the card, this instead triggers a special event rolled on the Daily Event Chart printed on the large Specialty Town card for the Type of Town the Heroes are currently in.

## UNWANTED ATTENTION

Unwanted Attention markers represent the Hero attracting the attention of a shady element in Town. This could be a drunk gambler that feels cheated or a surly merchant that doesn’t like the way you look.

### Gaining Unwanted Attention

There are two main ways to gain Unwanted Attention markers in Town:

Too Many Mutations - When a Hero enters Town, they gain 1 Unwanted Attention marker for every Mutation they have over 3. (Ex. 4 Mutations = 1 marker).

Gaining Gold at the Saloon / Gambling Hall - At the end of any Location visit to the Saloon or Gambling Hall in which a Hero gains any amount of Gold, for any reason, they also gain 1 Unwanted Attention marker.

### When Trouble Comes a Callin’

While a Hero has 1 or more Unwanted Attention markers, any time they roll a 7 on a Location Event Chart, they must roll to see if they are attacked! Roll a D6. If the roll is equal to or less than the number of Unwanted Attention markers they have, the Hero is attacked from behind by a cowardly but dangerous individual, with murder on the mind!

The Hero must immediately make a Luck 5+ test to move aside at the last minute. If successful, the would-be- assassin runs off into a back alley. If failed, the Hero is shot in the back, taking D6 x Hero Level Wounds, ignoring Defense and Armor. If this KO’s the Hero, they are killed!

All Unwanted Attention markers are removed at the end of a Hero’s Town Stay.

## EXPANDED BOUNTY

As the Darkness spreads and more demons and creatures flood into the area, the Frontier Outposts across the region begin to post more varied Bounties based on the current ‘Most Wanted’ monsters.

When determining the Bounty at the Frontier Outpost Location in Town, the Hero may either roll on the entire chart as normal, or choose to draw a Low Threat card instead.  If they choose to draw a card, the new Bounty covers every Enemy listed on that Threat card, and the Gold value is equal to $10 x the Health of that Enemy. If the Enemy has a normal XP value, the Hero that kills that Enemy gains the Bounty. If the Enemy has a variable XP value (XP +5 per Wound), then ALL Heroes gain the Bounty when that Enemy is defeated.

Note that the Sheriff’s Office Location also has a Sheriff's Bounty available, which is a little different. This is separate from the normal Frontier Outpost Bounty and it is possible to have both of these Bounties at the same time (imagine it thematically as the Frontier Outpost Bounty is for the whole region while the Sheriff’s Bounty is paid out by that particular Town).

## SPECIALTY TOWN TYPES

The large Specialty Town cards allow the Heroes to find more specific Types of Towns, each with its own feel and inherent benefits and drawbacks. When one of these Specialty Towns is discovered by the Heroes, use the large card for that Type of Town to find the Special Rules, Daily Event Chart, and Extra Options that the Heroes have during their Town Stay.

### Special Rules

Each Specialty Town Type has a list of Special Rules that are in effect as long as the Heroes are at that Type of Town. These Special Rules add extra theme and flavor specific to being in the listed Type of Town.

#### Daily Events

Each Specialty Town card also has a chart of Daily Events that are themed for that Type of Town. These Daily Events do not happen every Day in Town, but rather they are triggered by the Daily Event card Just Another Day on the Frontier, as previously noted in the Daily Event Deck section.

#### Extra Options

The Extra Options section of a Specialty Town card lists additional activities and Items the Heroes may find while in that Type of Town. If there is a specific Location that a Hero needs to be at to purchase these items or take part in an activity, it will be listed in that entry. Otherwise, the Hero may use the Extra Option regardless of the Location they visit during their Day in Town.

## ‘WANTED!’

While visiting Town, it is now possible for a Hero to find that they have become ‘Wanted’ by the local authorities. This is usually the result of engaging in illegal activities in Town, or being in the wrong place at the wrong time for certain Location Events. When a Hero becomes ‘Wanted’, they take one of the ‘Wanted’ reference cards that lists the effects of their new status. If a Hero is already ‘Wanted’ and they would gain the ‘Wanted’ status again, they instead become ‘Most Wanted’; the effects of which are listed on the back of the normal ‘Wanted’ reference card.

This ‘Wanted’ status continues to affect a Hero until it is removed. A Hero may visit the Sheriff’s Office Location in any Town to Pay Off Your Warrants and remove their ‘Wanted’ status, as detailed on the Sheriff’s Office Location card.

## THE DEVIL’S WHEEL

The Devil’s Wheel is a mini-game that can be played by Heroes that are visiting the Gambling Hall Town Location, and can be found on the Location Chart for the Gambling Hall.

### Setting Up

To set up The Devil’s Wheel, take the Frontier Town Expansion Box Top and lay it face down on a flat surface.

Then take 3 Random Mine Artifact cards (without looking at them) and lay them face down in the Box Top in a triangle around the center of the box. Lastly, place the Devil’s Head token at the center of the box, overlapping the 3 Mine Artifact cards, with the small dice icons each ‘pointing’ to an Artifact.

### Playing The Devil’s Wheel

To play a round of The Devil’s Wheel, roll 3 dice into the Box Top. These dice must bounce off of at least one inner wall of the Box Top. The dice are scored based on where they land in the box:

Any die that lands partially on the Mine Artifact cards is worth Points equal to the number rolled on that die.

Any die that is entirely on the Mine Artifact cards and/or the Devil’s Head token is worth double the value rolled on the die.

All other dice are worth no Points.

After the first roll, any number of dice (player’s choice) may be picked up and rolled a second time. After the second roll, any number of the dice (again, player’s choice) may be picked up and rolled for a third and final time. Note that for each of these rolls, the dice must always bounce off of at least one inner wall of the box and dice not re-rolled during the second or third roll are left in the box and may be moved or changed if hit by the new rolls.

Once the 9 rolling is complete, total the number of Points for the dice in the box to see your final score!

Note: The player may NOT use Grit or any other Items or abilities to Re-roll any of the dice rolled while playing The Devil’s Wheel.

### Scoring

The Hero is paid winnings based on how many Points they earned for their final score at the end of the round. This can be found on the Location Chart for the Gambling Hall Town Location.

### The Devil’s Head Jackpot

Once the round is complete and Points have been totaled, for each die that sits at least partially on the Devil’s Head token, the Hero may press their luck and try for a Devil’s Head Jackpot!

Roll the die. On a 1, 2, or 3, the Hero is bitten by the Devil and gains that many Corruption Points, ignoring Willpower! On the roll of a 4, 5, or 6, the Hero wins the Jackpot and may take the Mine Artifact card ‘pointed to’ by that number on the token. (Immediately replace any Artifacts won with new ones from the Artifact deck to re-set for the next Round.)

In addition, any time an Artifact is collected from The Devil’s Wheel like this, it also triggers a Jackpot for the whole room! This gives D6 × $25 to every other Hero currently at the Gambling Hall Location with you.

## BANDITS

Bandits are a new Enemy Type introduced in the Frontier Town Expansion. There are 6 Bandit models included. Cattle rustlers, horse thieves, and bank robbers; Bandits are rough and rowdy outlaws. They are not terribly tough, but can pack a serious punch with their hail of bullets from their six-shootin’ pistols or rooftop rifles!

### Shootout

Bandits prefer a running gun fight rather than closing in to get their hands dirty. Because their main focus is making Ranged Attacks, they will generally attempt to stay back, away from the Heroes, and attack them from a distance. To represent this, Bandits have the Standard Ability Shootout. The rules for Shootout are summarized on the Shootout Reference card, but are also covered here in greater detail.

### Ambushing

When a group of Enemies with the Shootout Standard Ability needs to be placed in Ambush, they are not placed adjacent to the Heroes like other Enemies. Instead, select a Random Hero and place all of the Shootout Enemies from that group on the selected Hero’s Map Tile, as far from any Heroes as possible. This usually allows them to sneak in from a side passage or get placed at the end of a hall or room, maybe even behind the Heroes. If there are not enough empty spaces on that Map Tile, any remaining Enemies are placed as far from the Heroes as possible on any Map Tiles adjacent to the selected Hero.

### Retreat

When an Enemy with Shootout starts its Activation adjacent to one or more Heroes, it will usually try to move away. Roll a D6 for the Enemy model. On the roll of 4, 5, or 6, the Enemy immediately makes a Free Move along the shortest distance to no longer be adjacent to any Heroes. This Free Move is outside of the Enemy’s normal Move and does not count against its Movement allowance for the turn.

### Melee Attacks

Any Shootout Enemy that failed to Retreat (or was stuck adjacent to a Hero, and could not move away), will make a normal Combat Attack against a Random adjacent Hero this turn, instead of their Ranged Attack.

### Shootout Markers

Shootout markers are used to denote which Heroes have been targeted, and by how many Enemy Ranged Attacks. Any suitable marker or token may be used for this. Shootout markers are a simple way to keep track of which Heroes have already been targeted, and by how many of the Enemies.

### Targeting

After any Retreat moves, the Shootout Enemies that are not adjacent to a Hero, will each need a Target for their Ranged Attacks. Step through the Shootout Enemies, one by one, completing the following procedure for each of them.

Select a Random Hero that does not have a Shootout marker on him yet (or that has the fewest Shootout markers if all Heroes already have one) and move the shortest distance to get within Range and Line of Sight to that Target. The Enemy MAY move through other Enemies from its group during this movement, as they make way for one another.

At the end of this Move, if the selected Target is within Range and Line of Sight, place a Shootout marker on that Target. If not, select a new Random Hero that is currently within Range and Line of Sight to the Enemy (prioritizing those with the fewest Shootout markers) and place a Shootout marker on him instead.

Then proceed to the next Enemy in the group.

### Making Ranged Attacks

Once all of the Enemy Group has moved and placed their Shootout markers, roll Ranged Attacks from the Enemies against each Hero equal to the number of Shootout markers on that Hero. Then, remove all of the Shootout markers.

### Rooftop Bandits with Rifles

Sometimes during a Mission, one or more Bandits will be placed onto a Building Rooftop. When this happens, the Bandit comes into play with a Bandit Rifle Ranged Attack instead of his normal Bandit Pistols. This is covered more in the Rooftops section below, as well as in the specific Mission itself, though it is important to note that Rooftop Bandits will never leave the Rooftop, making it easy to remember that if a Bandit is on a Rooftop, he has a Bandit Rifle.

### Infamous Bandit Gangs

Some Bandits aren’t ordinary outlaws, but rather an Infamous Bandit Gang, wanted throughout the land for their dirty deeds. When this occurs (usually called for by a Threat card), draw an Infamous Bandit Gang card to see which Gang the Heroes have encountered. Infamous Bandits use the same models and Enemy Record Sheet for normal Bandits, but with some special rules and abilities listed on the card.

Only 1 Infamous Bandit Gang card may be in play at a time, and all Bandits are part of that Gang while the card is in play (discard at the end of the fight it was drawn for).

Infamous Bandit Gang cards include the name, story, and special rules/Abilities for members of that Gang, as well as the bonus XP and Gold (Bounty) a Hero gets for killing a member of that Gang!

## TOWN ADVENTURE BOARD

The Town Adventure Board allows the Heroes to have Adventures in the Town itself! There are 6 Missions included in this Adventure Book for use with this new game board.

### Using the Town Adventure Board

Missions that use the Town Adventure Board will list it in the Set Up section of the Mission itself. Unfold the board and place it in the center of the table where all players can reach it.

There is no Exploration on the Town Adventure Board as it is a fixed-map. Also, it is assumed that the entire Town is well lit and the Heroes do not need to worry about the Lantern’s Light or Voices in the Dark during Missions on the Town Adventure Board. One Hero should still have the Old Lantern to make Hold Back the Darkness rolls. The Town Depth Event Chart can be found on one of the large Town Reference cards included. Unless noted otherwise in the Mission, Heroes may NOT Scavenge on the Town Adventure Board and any time a Growing Dread card would need to be drawn, it is revealed and played immediately.

The Town Adventure Board is split into 8 individual Buildings and a large Street Area (all of the outdoor spaces not within a Building). Each of these counts as its own Map Tile for game effects.

### The Street Area

All of the spaces on the board that are not inside of a Building are considered part of the Street Area. This Street Area counts as 1 large Map Tile.

### Buildings

There are 8 different Buildings on the board, and each is numbered in the corner. These Building numbers are used to reference particular Buildings during the Adventure (usually during Set Up) and are also used when the Players need to roll a Random Building. Simply roll the D8 and find that numbered Building on the board.

As noted above, each Building (as well as the Street Area) counts as a distinct Map Tile for game effects. This means that if an Enemy in the Street Area causes Terror, a Hero may run inside of a Building to no longer be on the monsters’ Map Tile, thus avoiding the Horror Hits. It is also commonly used for one Hero to drag another Hero that is KO’d into a Building that has no Enemies inside so that he may help that Hero to Recover and get back into the action.

### Walls

Each Building is enclosed in a thick, black line that represents the Walls. Walls block movement and Line of Sight (just like the Walls on the outer edges of a Mine Map Tile). Unless there is a Window or Door between two spaces, a Wall between two spaces means that those two spaces DO NOT count as being adjacent to each other for game effects (such as needing an Escape test to move away from an Enemy on the other side of the Wall, using a Saloon Girl’s Comforting Presence, etc). Walls also block things such as Dynamite from bouncing through them. Any model that is XL or larger may ignore Walls completely during its movement (smashing through them).

### Doors

Doors are open sections of a Wall (between two spaces) that allow models to move through that Wall. A model may move straight through a Door during normal movement, but may NOT move diagonally through a Door.

Unless a model is standing in one of the two spaces touching a Door, the Door blocks Line of Sight, just like a Wall (it is assumed the Door is shut).

A model standing on one side of a Door may see out and see the map beyond the Line of Sight Arc of that Door (shown in the diagram below). This model counts as being in Cover from any Attacks made from the other side of the Door (as detailed below).

### Windows

The Windows of a Building are marked as light blue sections of Wall. Like Doors, a model standing on one side of a Window may see out and be seen through the Line of Sight Arc of that Window (shown in the diagram below). This model counts as being in Cover from any Attacks made from the other side of the Window (as detailed below).

In this way, for Line of Sight, Windows work just like Doors. Unlike Doors, models may not normally move through Windows. However, during a Hero’s Move, they are allowed to spend 1 Grit to jump through a Window and continue moving. This does not cost any additional movement points, it simply allows them to move through a Window just like it was a Door.

### Diagonal Doors

There are two Buildings on the board that have a special Diagonal Door facing out into the street intersection. These two Diagonal Doors work slightly differently than the rest of the Doors on the map, as the Diagonal Door is in a single space rather than being between two spaces, like a normal Door.

A Diagonal Door space counts as both inside the Building as well as out in the Street Area, similar to a puzzle-cut connection space between Map Tiles down in the Mines (it counts as being on both “Map Tiles”). A model in a Diagonal Door space must choose to either be facing into the Building or out into the streets. Face the model accordingly to represent this. A model in a Diagonal Door space counts as being in Cover against any Attacks made from the direction they are facing – either from inside the Building or from the Streets (as detailed below). ### Cover

When a model is in Cover, it gains some protection from attacks, getting a special Cover Save against all incoming Hits.

### Cover Save

A model that is in Cover is allowed to make a special Cover roll any time they are Hit by an Attack. Roll a D6 for each Hit the model just took. On the roll of 4, 5, or 6, they ignore that Hit entirely. Cover saves like this are made before any normal Defense rolls. Note that Cover has no effect on Horror Hits or Corruption Hits, only regular Hits.

A model does NOT get a Cover save against an Attack made from an adjacent space, as it is assumed that the attacker and target are close enough to attack each other around the Cover. Hits from Dynamite, or markers placed in a model’s space (such as Hellfire markers), also ignore Cover.

### Enemies Using Cover

When an Enemy with Shootout (such as a Bandit) selects its target to Attack, roll a D8. On the roll of 1, 2, or 3, that Enemy model will attempt to take Cover before attacking. If the Enemy model has a place that it could move into Cover and still be within Line of Sight and Range to its selected target, it will; if there are multiple options for Cover, the Enemy moves to the Cover closest to its current position to still be in Range and Line of Sight. On the roll of 4, 5, or 6, the Enemy will ignore any Cover and move to Attack as normal for Shootout.

If an Enemy with Shootout starts its Activation already in Cover, it will select a target only from those that are already in Line of Sight and Range from its current position (in other words, if an Enemy is already in Cover, it will always try and stay there until it has to move away to be able to Attack).

### Leaning Around Corners and Doors

Another way that a model may take Cover is to Lean Around a Corner or Lean Out of a Doorway.

Any model that is in a space at the outside corner of a Building Wall, may spend 1 movement point to move onto the line between the space they are in and the space in front of them. This is called Splitting the Space, and represents that model Leaning Around the Corner to make Ranged Attacks. The model counts as being in both spaces at once for all purposes. Count Range to or from the model from either of the spaces they are in. Face the model in the direction they are Leaning Around the Corner in. A model Leaning Around a Corner counts as being in Cover against any Attacks made from the direction they are facing (imagine a line extending out from the Building Wall - the model has Cover against any Attacks coming from the other side of that line).

Likewise, a model that is in a space touching a Door may spend 1 Movement Point to Lean In or Out of that Doorway. Move the model into the line the Doorway is on ‘to split the space’ and face the model either into the Building or out into the Street Area. The model counts as being in both spaces that are touching the Door at once and gains Cover from any Attacks made from the direction they are facing (either from inside the Building or from the Streets).

### Rooftops

All of the Buildings on the Town Adventure Board also have a Rooftop that is accessible to models, allowing them to climb up high to get a better vantage point on the Town. Buildings 1, 2, 5, and 6 are all individual Rooftops, while Buildings 3 and 4 share a single Rooftop that includes the footprint of both Buildings combined. Similarly, Buildings 7 and 8 also share a single Rooftop.

When a model is on a Rooftop, it is represented by creating a stack of 10 or so large counters (such as the 5-piece Sanity or Wound markers) and placing the model on top of the stack to show that they are now high above the ground.

A model on a Rooftop is no longer in any particular space, but is rather on the entire Rooftop as a whole. All models on the same Rooftop count as being adjacent to one another, and any number of models may be on the same Rooftop.

A model on any Rooftop counts as being in the Street Area “Map Tile”.

Any model on a Rooftop counts as being in Cover from all attacks, except those from other models on the same Rooftop.

### Rooftops and Line of Sight

Line of Sight to and from a model on a Rooftop is counted from the Building space closest to the target/attacker.

A model on a Rooftop always has Line of Sight to all other Rooftops on the Board, but may NOT draw Line of Sight through other Building Walls to see a target on the ground.

A model on a Rooftop does NOT have Line of Sight to or from any space within the Building below, or to any spaces directly adjacent to that Building (touching one of its Walls).

### Moving to and from a Rooftop

A Hero that starts his Activation inside of a Building, may give up his movement for the turn to move up to the Rooftop. Place the Hero model on a high stack of large counters (as noted above) and move the model to be centrally located in the Building to show that they are now on the Rooftop. A Hero that is already on a Rooftop may give up their movement to move back down into the Building. Remove the stack of counters from under the model and place the model in any space of that Building (or in any space of either Building if it is a shared Rooftop).

Enemies will only move up to the Rooftop of a building if the Hero they are targeting is already up there and they need to move to be adjacent for a Melee Attack. If the Enemy ends its Move in the building, it is automatically moved to the Rooftop with its target, but loses its Attack for this Activation.

Enemies with the Shootout Standard Ability (such as Bandits) will never enter from a Hero on the same Rooftop, but instead will stay and always Melee Attack that Hero. A Hero on a Rooftop with one or more Enemies must make an Escape test to leave that Rooftop.

### Bandits on Rooftops

In several of the Town Adventure Missions, some Bandits will enter play on Rooftops. These Bandits come into play with a Bandit Rifle instead of their Bandit Pistols (the details for these can be found in the Shootout in the Streets Mission). These Rooftop Bandits will never leave their Rooftop throughout the game.

All Rooftop Bandits in play should Activate and make their Shootout Attacks as a group before Activating any other Bandits that are on the ground. This helps to keep the Bandit Rifle Attacks separate from the Bandit Pistol Attacks.

### Falling Off of a Rooftop

Whenever an Enemy on a Rooftop is killed by a Hero, roll a D6. On the roll of 1, 2, or 3, the Enemy falls from the Rooftop screaming and the Hero may Recover 1 Grit.

Whenever a Hero on a Rooftop takes 1 or more Wounds from an Attack, roll a D6. On the roll of 1, 2, or 3, the Hero falls from the Rooftop! Place the Hero in any ground space adjacent to the Building and the Hero takes D6 Wounds ignoring Defense.

### Flying Models and Buildings

Any model that has a Flight ability may move over Buildings during its movement (ignoring Walls as it goes) to reach a target on the other side or on the Rooftop (if on the Rooftop, place the model on a stack of counters at the end of its move to show that it is now on the Rooftop as well). Unlike normal Enemies moving to Rooftops, Flying Enemies do NOT lose their Attack the turn they move to the Rooftop. To reach a target inside a Building, the model must still move through a Door to get inside.

### Dynamite and Buildings

Dynamite may NOT be thrown or Bounce through Walls, except where there is a Door or Window; and then it may only be targeted at the space directly on the other side of that Door/Window (just like Line of Sight for Doors/Windows with normal Ranged Attacks). Dynamite that goes off next to a Wall WILL still hit every adjacent space, even through a Wall. Dynamite that goes off in/near a Building will not affect models on the Rooftop.

Dynamite MAY be thrown onto a Rooftop. When thrown, declare that the Rooftop is the target and place the Dynamite on any space of the Building within Range (as it is lobbed upward). As long as the Dynamite does not Bounce out of a space of that Building, it will go off, hitting every model on that Rooftop! If the Dynamite Bounces outside of a Building space, then it will land on the ground in whatever space it ends up in, ignoring models on the Rooftop and hitting models on the ground level instead.

### Entry Areas

Along each of the 4 board edges there is a marked Entry Area (A, B, C, and D), highlighted in blue. Each of these Entry Areas covers 3 spaces.

Entry Areas are often used for models to enter or exit the board. When new enemies are placed at an Entry Area, they always get placed in the center space of that Entry Area first, then the two adjacent spaces of the Entry Area. It is also common for models to move onto or off of the board at an Entry Area. When this happens, simply continue the movement of that model, starting in any of the 3 Entry Area spaces. When a model moves off the board (generally only as part of a specific Mission rule), the model moves into any of the Entry Area spaces, and then moves 1 more space to move off the Board.

The Frontier Town Expansion has 6 new Missions to challenge your Heroes. These can be played as special Missions triggered by In-Town events or simply mixed in to your current campaign to expand your Adventures into the Town itself.

D6 Roll — Frontier Town Missions

1 — Shootout in the Streets

### Playing a Mission in Town

Missions in Town work just like Missions down in the Mines; Heroes start the Adventure with 1 Grit as normal (and any Damage they might have from their previous Travel/Town Stay), one Hero takes the Old Lantern and makes Hold Back the Darkness tests, etc. To make any Town Mission more challenging, you may draw an Infamous Bandit Gang card at the start of the game and apply it to all Bandits in that Mission.

### After Completing a Mission in Town

At the end of a Mission in Town, Heroes do all of the normal things for completing a Mission; rolling for Dark Stone Corruption, Fully Heal Health and Sanity; return to 1 Grit, etc.

One difference is that as long as the Heroes did not Fail Mission, they have the option to stay in the current Town instead of Traveling to a new Town. If the Heroes decide to stay in the same Town, this new Town Stay will be in the same size and Specific Town Type that the Heroes visited most recently. Everything else for the Town should still be set up fresh (Random Town Locations, etc). If the Heroes did Fail the Town Mission, they must travel to a new Town.

As the morning of his public hanging arrives, the rest of the town looks on in despair. If only someone would stand up to these dirty law-men, and bring justice back to this town!

### Set Up

This Mission uses the Town Adventure Board. Place the Gallows token as well as 4 Bandits, and 10 Townsfolk markers on the board, as shown in the diagram below:

(diagram omitted but preserved as image)

In addition, if there are 3 or more Heroes in the Posse, place 2 more Bandits on Random Building Rooftops. Bandits on Rooftops have Bandit Rifles instead of Bandit Pistols, as described in the Shootout in the Streets Mission.

The Heroes may start in any of the spaces along the length of the entire Board Edge with the Entry Area marked ‘C’ on it.

### Mission Goal

The Corrupt Marshals are set to hang the innocent prisoner in a public execution! You can’t let that happen! The Heroes must either kill all of the Corrupt Marshals or free the prisoner from the noose and help him to escape.

### Special Rules - Corrupt Marshals

This Adventure uses the Bandit Enemies to represent the Corrupt Marshals. Corrupt Marshals use the Brutal side of the Enemy Record Sheet for Bandits (or the Brutal side plus ALL Elite Abilities if the Hero Posse is level 5 or higher), and gain an extra bonus based on the number of Heroes in the Hero Posse.

# of Heroes	Bandit Bonus
1–2	2× Health and Immune to Critical Hits
3–4	2× Health and Immune to Critical Hits, also 2× Rooftop Bandits (noted above)
5–6	3× Health and Immune to Critical Hits, also 2× Rooftop Bandits (noted above)

### Alerting the Corrupt Marshals

The Corrupt Marshals are standing guard and watching the crowds. They will not Activate until the turn after they have been Alerted to the Heroes’ presence.

The Marshals will all become Alerted as soon as any Hero makes an Attack.

Also, at the end of any Hero’s Move, if they are within 3 spaces and within Line of Sight to any Corrupt Marshal (including those on the Rooftops), roll a D6. On the roll of 1 or 2, the Marshals all become Alerted.

### The Gallows and Townsfolk Crowds

The Gallows marker takes up a 3×3 space area and blocks movement and Line of Sight passing through any of those spaces.

The 10 Townsfolk markers represent the crowds gathered to watch the hanging. These Townsfolk also block movement and Line of Sight from passing through their space, though they do not block Line of Sight to or from the Gallows or Rooftops. Models may move and trace Line of Sight diagonally between two Townsfolk Crowds that are touching corners. This allows Heroes to sneak up amongst the crowds to get close, without Alerting the Corrupt Marshals.

These Townsfolk Crowds will start to scatter once the shooting starts. After any model finishes making an Attack, any Townsfolk Crowd markers adjacent to it will immediately scatter (Bounce) 1 space in a Random Direction – use the Dynamite Bounce chart to do this. If the marker cannot move in that direction (because of another model or wall, etc), the Townsfolk marker simply stays where it is.

Townsfolk markers may not be attacked or targeted in any way. If a Townsfolk is in the Area of Effect of an Attack (for example, in the same or adjacent space to Dynamite or if a Hellfire marker is placed in their space), the Townsfolk marker is instantly removed and the model that made the Attack immediately gains D6 Corruption points, ignoring Willpower, for each Townsfolk removed in this way.

### The Countdown

This Mission does not use the Depth Track at all. Instead, at the start of the Adventure, take the number counters marked 1–8 and set them at the top of the board, stacked face up in order, with 1 at the bottom and 8 showing at the top of the stack. This is the Countdown Stack that marks how much time is left until the lever is pulled and the Prisoner is hanged!

At the end of each turn, remove the top counter from the stack.

Also, the Heroes should still roll a Hold Back the Darkness test each turn (still getting Depth Events on doubles), needing to roll 7+ for the duration of the Adventure. Any time the Hold Back the Darkness roll is failed, remove the top counter from the Countdown Stack as well.

When the last counter is removed from the Countdown Stack, the rope drops and the Prisoner is hung! At this point, the Heroes have one last chance to save the Prisoner by shooting out the rope and setting him free!

### Shooting the Rope

When the last Countdown marker is removed from the stack, the Heroes have one last chance to save the Prisoner from hanging. One Hero within Range and Line of Sight to the Gallows may take a single Shot with a Ranged Weapon outside of the normal turn sequence. Roll To Hit and for Damage as normal. The rope has no Defense, but the Hit must do 6 Wounds to successfully snap the rope.

If failed, the Prisoner is hung and the Mission is failed. If successful, the Prisoner is severed and the Prisoner is freed! Place the Escaped Prisoner marker in any empty space adjacent to the Gallows.

### Prisoner on the Run

If the Prisoner is set free by the Heroes shooting out the rope, the Escaped Prisoner marker is placed on the board. At the start of each of his turn (not including the turn he was freed), the Escaped Prisoner will move D6 spaces along the shortest path toward the Entry Point marked ‘C,’ to move off the board edge and get away.

The Escaped Prisoner blocks movement, but not Line of Sight, and will not Attack, as he is running for his life. He does not need to make Escape rolls to move past Enemies.

Once in play, the Escaped Prisoner is added as a potential target for the Corrupt Marshals (when rolling for a Random Hero, include the Escaped Prisoner).

If Attacked, he has Defense 4+ and 5 Health.

Any Hero on the board may use Grit to allow the Escaped Prisoner to Re-roll his Defense rolls.

### Objectives - Defeat the Corrupt Marshals

If ALL of the Corrupt Marshals are killed, or if the Prisoner is set free and is able to get away by moving off of the board at the Entry Area marked ‘C,’ the Mission is successfully completed.

### Corrupt Marshal Victory

The Mission is failed if the innocent Prisoner is hung, or if he escapes the Gallows, but is killed trying to get away.

### Reward

Every Hero gains 100 XP and may draw a Gear card. If all of the Corrupt Marshals were killed, also gain D6×$100.

### Failure

The Heroes may not visit Town, but must begin the next Adventure directly. All Heroes gain D3 Corruption Points, ignoring Willpower, from the guilt of their failure.

### Optional Rules

Using this Mission to Rescue a Hero

Sometimes a Hero in Town will be arrested (usually trying to rob the bank), and set to hang! Any time a Hero in Town would be killed by hanging, the rest of the Hero’s Posse may play this Mission to try and rescue him. During Set Up, place the captive Hero model on the center space of the Gallows token. He takes no part in the game until set free.

The Mission works exactly as written above, except that if the Rope is shot out and the Prisoner is freed, the captive Hero is immediately placed in any space adjacent to the Gallows instead of the Escaped Prisoner marker. The captive Hero does not have any of his Items or Side Bag Tokens (except those that cannot be discarded or sold, such as Parasites). Instead, he starts with 1 Grit and a simple Starting Gear Pistol tossed to him by someone in the crowd.

Starting in the turn after he is released, the captive Hero may Activate at his Initiative as normal. The Mission is failed if the captive Hero is KO’d (also, if KO’d, the captive Hero may only roll a single D6 on the Injury Chart). The Mission is won if the captive Hero moves off the board edge at the Entry Area marked ‘C,’ or if all of the Corrupt Marshals are killed.

Either way, the captive Hero regains all of his Items and Tokens at the end of the Adventure.

The expanded Town material in this expansion greatly

Small, Medium, and Large Towns

Travel to Town, the first thing they must now do is decide

Expanded Travel Chart

The Daily Event Deck adds a new element to your Town

One common Daily Event card that has several copies in

UNWANTED ATTENTION

Unwanted Attention markers represent the
Hero attracting the attention of a shady element in
Town. This could be a drunk gambler that feels
cheated or a surly merchant that doesn’t like the
way you look.

Gaining Unwanted Attention

There are two main ways to gain Unwanted Attention
markers in Town:

Too Many Mutations - When a Hero enters Town, they
gain 1 Unwanted Attention marker for every Mutation
they have over 3. (Ex. 4 Mutations = 1 marker).

Gaining Gold at the Saloon / Gambling Hall - At the
end of any Location visit to the Saloon or Gambling

for any reason, they also gain 1 Unwanted Attention
marker.

When Trouble Comes a Callin’

While a Hero has 1 or more Unwanted Attention
markers, any time they roll a 7 on a Location Event Chart,
they must roll to see if they are attacked! Roll a D6. If
the roll is equal to or less than the number of Unwanted
Attention markers they have, the Hero is attacked from
behind by a cowardly but dangerous individual, with
murder on the mind!

The Hero must immediately make a Luck 5+ test to
move aside at the last minute. If successful, the would-be-
assassin runs off into a back alley. If failed, the Hero is shot
in the back, taking D6 x Hero Level Wounds, ignoring
Defense and Armor. If this KO’s the Hero, they are killed!

All Unwanted Attention markers are removed
at the end of a Hero’s Town Stay.

THE DEVIL’S WHEEL

The Devil’s Wheel is a mini-game that can be played by Heroes that are visiting the Gambling Hall Town Location, and can be found on the Location Chart for the Gambling Hall.

Setting Up

To set up The Devil’s Wheel, take the Frontier Town Expansion Box Top and lay it face down on a flat surface.

Then take 3 Random Mine Artifact cards (without looking at them) and lay them face down in the Box Top in a triangle around the center of the box. Lastly, place the Devil’s Head token at the center of the box, overlapping the 3 Mine Artifact cards, with the small dice icons each ‘pointing’ to an Artifact.

Playing The Devil’s Wheel

To play a round of The Devil’s Wheel, roll 3 dice into the Box Top. These dice must bounce off of at least one inner wall of the Box Top. The dice are scored based on where they land in the box:

Any die that lands partially on the Mine Artifact cards is worth Points equal to the number rolled on that die.

Any die that is entirely on the Mine Artifact cards and/or the Devil’s Head token is worth double the value rolled on the die.

All other dice are worth no Points.

After the first roll, any number of dice (player’s choice) may be picked up and rolled a second time. After the second roll, any number of the dice (again, player’s choice) may be picked up and rolled for a third and final time. Note that for each of these rolls, the dice must always bounce off of at least one inner wall of the box and dice not re-rolled during the second or third roll are left in the box and may be moved or changed if hit by the new rolls.

Once the 9 rolling is complete, total the number of Points for the dice in the box to see your final score!

Note: The player may NOT use Grit or any other Items or abilities to Re-roll any of the dice rolled while playing The Devil’s Wheel.

Scoring

The Hero is paid winnings based on how many Points they earned for their final score at the end of the round. This can be found on the Location Chart for the Gambling Hall Town Location.

The Devil’s Head Jackpot

Once the round is complete and Points have been totaled, for each die that sits at least partially on the Devil’s Head token, the Hero may press their luck and try for a Devil’s Head Jackpot!

Roll the die. On a 1, 2, or 3, the Hero is bitten by the Devil and gains that many Corruption Points, ignoring Willpower! On the roll of a 4, 5, or 6, the Hero wins the Jackpot and may take the Mine Artifact card ‘pointed to’ by that number on the token. (Immediately replace any Artifacts won with new ones from the Artifact deck to re-set for the next Round.)

In addition, any time an Artifact is collected from The Devil’s Wheel like this, it also triggers a Jackpot for the whole room! This gives D6 × $25 to every other Hero currently at the Gambling Hall Location with you.

BANDITS

Bandits are a new Enemy Type introduced in the Frontier Town Expansion. There are 6 Bandit models included. Cattle rustlers, horse thieves, and bank robbers; Bandits are rough and rowdy outlaws. They are not terribly tough, but can pack a serious punch with their hail of bullets from their six-shootin’ pistols or rooftop rifles!

Shootout

Bandits prefer a running gun fight rather than closing in to get their hands dirty. Because their main focus is making Ranged Attacks, they will generally attempt to stay back, away from the Heroes, and attack them from a distance. To represent this, Bandits have the Standard Ability Shootout. The rules for Shootout are summarized on the Shootout Reference card, but are also covered here in greater detail.

Ambushing

When a group of Enemies with the Shootout Standard Ability needs to be placed in Ambush, they are not placed adjacent to the Heroes like other Enemies. Instead, select a Random Hero and place all of the Shootout Enemies from that group on the selected Hero’s Map Tile, as far from any Heroes as possible. This usually allows them to sneak in from a side passage or get placed at the end of a hall or room, maybe even behind the Heroes. If there are not enough empty spaces on that Map Tile, any remaining Enemies are placed as far from the Heroes as possible on any Map Tiles adjacent to the selected Hero.

Retreat

When an Enemy with Shootout starts its Activation adjacent to one or more Heroes, it will usually try to move away. Roll a D6 for the Enemy model. On the roll of 4, 5, or 6, the Enemy immediately makes a Free Move along the shortest distance to no longer be adjacent to any Heroes. This Free Move is outside of the Enemy’s normal Move and does not count against its Movement allowance for the turn.

Melee Attacks

Any Shootout Enemy that failed to Retreat (or was stuck adjacent to a Hero, and could not move away), will make a normal Combat Attack against a Random adjacent Hero this turn, instead of their Ranged Attack.

Shootout Markers

Shootout markers are used to denote which Heroes have been targeted, and by how many Enemy Ranged Attacks. Any suitable marker or token may be used for this. Shootout markers are a simple way to keep track of which Heroes have already been targeted, and by how many of the Enemies.

Targeting

After any Retreat moves, the Shootout Enemies that are not adjacent to a Hero, will each need a Target for their Ranged Attacks. Step through the Shootout Enemies, one by one, completing the following procedure for each of them.

Select a Random Hero that does not have a Shootout marker on him yet (or that has the fewest Shootout markers if all Heroes already have one) and move the shortest distance to get within Range and Line of Sight to that Target. The Enemy MAY move through other Enemies from its group during this movement, as they make way for one another.

At the end of this Move, if the selected Target is within Range and Line of Sight, place a Shootout marker on that Target. If not, select a new Random Hero that is currently within Range and Line of Sight to the Enemy (prioritizing those with the fewest Shootout markers) and place a Shootout marker on him instead.

Then proceed to the next Enemy in the group.

Making Ranged Attacks

Once all of the Enemy Group has moved and placed their Shootout markers, roll Ranged Attacks from the Enemies against each Hero equal to the number of Shootout markers on that Hero. Then, remove all of the Shootout markers.

Rooftop Bandits with Rifles

Sometimes during a Mission, one or more Bandits will be placed onto a Building Rooftop. When this happens, the Bandit comes into play with a Bandit Rifle Ranged Attack instead of his normal Bandit Pistols. This is covered more in the Rooftops section below, as well as in the specific Mission itself, though it is important to note that Rooftop Bandits will never leave the Rooftop, making it easy to remember that if a Bandit is on a Rooftop, he has a Bandit Rifle.

Infamous Bandit Gangs

Some Bandits aren’t ordinary outlaws, but rather an Infamous Bandit Gang, wanted throughout the land for their dirty deeds. When this occurs (usually called for by a Threat card), draw an Infamous Bandit Gang card to see which Gang the Heroes have encountered. Infamous Bandits use the same models and Enemy Record Sheet for normal Bandits, but with some special rules and abilities listed on the card.

Only 1 Infamous Bandit Gang card may be in play at a time, and all Bandits are part of that Gang while the card is in play (discard at the end of the fight it was drawn for).

Infamous Bandit Gang cards include the name, story, and special rules/Abilities for members of that Gang, as well as the bonus XP and Gold (Bounty) a Hero gets for killing a member of that Gang!

TOWN ADVENTURE BOARD

The Town Adventure Board allows the Heroes to have Adventures in the Town itself! There are 6 Missions included in this Adventure Book for use with this new game board.

Using the Town Adventure Board

Missions that use the Town Adventure Board will list it in the Set Up section of the Mission itself. Unfold the board and place it in the center of the table where all players can reach it.

There is no Exploration on the Town Adventure Board as it is a fixed-map. Also, it is assumed that the entire Town is well lit and the Heroes do not need to worry about the Lantern’s Light or Voices in the Dark during Missions on the Town Adventure Board. One Hero should still have the Old Lantern to make Hold Back the Darkness rolls. The Town Depth Event Chart can be found on one of the large Town Reference cards included. Unless noted otherwise in the Mission, Heroes may NOT Scavenge on the Town Adventure Board and any time a Growing Dread card would need to be drawn, it is revealed and played immediately.

The Town Adventure Board is split into 8 individual Buildings and a large Street Area (all of the outdoor spaces not within a Building). Each of these counts as its own Map Tile for game effects.

The Street Area

All of the spaces on the board that are not inside of a Building are considered part of the Street Area. This Street Area counts as 1 large Map Tile.

Buildings

There are 8 different Buildings on the board, and each is numbered in the corner. These Building numbers are used to reference particular Buildings during the Adventure (usually during Set Up) and are also used when the Players need to roll a Random Building. Simply roll the D8 and find that numbered Building on the board.

As noted above, each Building (as well as the Street Area) counts as a distinct Map Tile for game effects. This means that if an Enemy in the Street Area causes Terror, a Hero may run inside of a Building to no longer be on the monsters’ Map Tile, thus avoiding the Horror Hits. It is also commonly used for one Hero to drag another Hero that is KO’d into a Building that has no Enemies inside so that he may help that Hero to Recover and get back into the action.

Walls

Each Building is enclosed in a thick, black line that represents the Walls. Walls block movement and Line of Sight (just like the Walls on the outer edges of a Mine Map Tile). Unless there is a Window or Door between two spaces, a Wall between two spaces means that those two spaces DO NOT count as being adjacent to each other for game effects (such as needing an Escape test to move away from an Enemy on the other side of the Wall, using a Saloon Girl’s Comforting Presence, etc). Walls also block things such as Dynamite from bouncing through them. Any model that is XL or larger may ignore Walls completely during its movement (smashing through them).

Doors

Doors are open sections of a Wall (between two spaces) that allow models to move through that Wall. A model may move straight through a Door during normal movement, but may NOT move diagonally through a Door.

Unless a model is standing in one of the two spaces touching a Door, the Door blocks Line of Sight, just like a Wall (it is assumed the Door is shut).

A model standing on one side of a Door may see out and see the map beyond the Line of Sight Arc of that Door (shown in the diagram below). This model counts as being in Cover from any Attacks made from the other side of the Door (as detailed below).

Windows

The Windows of a Building are marked as light blue sections of Wall. Like Doors, a model standing on one side of a Window may see out and be seen through the Line of Sight Arc of that Window (shown in the diagram below). This model counts as being in Cover from any Attacks made from the other side of the Window (as detailed below).

In this way, for Line of Sight, Windows work just like Doors. Unlike Doors, models may not normally move through Windows. However, during a Hero’s Move, they are allowed to spend 1 Grit to jump through a Window and continue moving. This does not cost any additional movement points, it simply allows them to move through a Window just like it was a Door.

Diagonal Doors

There are two Buildings on the board that have a special Diagonal Door facing out into the street intersection. These two Diagonal Doors work slightly differently than the rest of the Doors on the map, as the Diagonal Door is in a single space rather than being between two spaces, like a normal Door.

A Diagonal Door space counts as both inside the Building as well as out in the Street Area, similar to a puzzle-cut connection space between Map Tiles down in the Mines (it counts as being on both “Map Tiles”). A model in a Diagonal Door space must choose to either be facing into the Building or out into the streets. Face the model accordingly to represent this. A model in a Diagonal Door space counts as being in Cover against any Attacks made from the direction they are facing – either from inside the Building or from the Streets (as detailed below). Cover

When a model is in Cover, it gains some protection from attacks, getting a special Cover Save against all incoming Hits.

Cover Save

A model that is in Cover is allowed to make a special Cover roll any time they are Hit by an Attack. Roll a D6 for each Hit the model just took. On the roll of 4, 5, or 6, they ignore that Hit entirely. Cover saves like this are made before any normal Defense rolls. Note that Cover has no effect on Horror Hits or Corruption Hits, only regular Hits.

A model does NOT get a Cover save against an Attack made from an adjacent space, as it is assumed that the attacker and target are close enough to attack each other around the Cover. Hits from Dynamite, or markers placed in a model’s space (such as Hellfire markers), also ignore Cover.

Enemies Using Cover

When an Enemy with Shootout (such as a Bandit) selects its target to Attack, roll a D8. On the roll of 1, 2, or 3, that Enemy model will attempt to take Cover before attacking. If the Enemy model has a place that it could move into Cover and still be within Line of Sight and Range to its selected target, it will; if there are multiple options for Cover, the Enemy moves to the Cover closest to its current position to still be in Range and Line of Sight. On the roll of 4, 5, or 6, the Enemy will ignore any Cover and move to Attack as normal for Shootout.

If an Enemy with Shootout starts its Activation already in Cover, it will select a target only from those that are already in Line of Sight and Range from its current position (in other words, if an Enemy is already in Cover, it will always try and stay there until it has to move away to be able to Attack).

Leaning Around Corners and Doors

Another way that a model may take Cover is to Lean Around a Corner or Lean Out of a Doorway.

Any model that is in a space at the outside corner of a Building Wall, may spend 1 movement point to move onto the line between the space they are in and the space in front of them. This is called Splitting the Space, and represents that model Leaning Around the Corner to make Ranged Attacks. The model counts as being in both spaces at once for all purposes. Count Range to or from the model from either of the spaces they are in. Face the model in the direction they are Leaning Around the Corner in. A model Leaning Around a Corner counts as being in Cover against any Attacks made from the direction they are facing (imagine a line extending out from the Building Wall - the model has Cover against any Attacks coming from the other side of that line).

Likewise, a model that is in a space touching a Door may spend 1 Movement Point to Lean In or Out of that Doorway. Move the model into the line the Doorway is on ‘to split the space’ and face the model either into the Building or out into the Street Area. The model counts as being in both spaces that are touching the Door at once and gains Cover from any Attacks made from the direction they are facing (either from inside the Building or from the Streets).

Rooftops

All of the Buildings on the Town Adventure Board also have a Rooftop that is accessible to models, allowing them to climb up high to get a better vantage point on the Town. Buildings 1, 2, 5, and 6 are all individual Rooftops, while Buildings 3 and 4 share a single Rooftop that includes the footprint of both Buildings combined. Similarly, Buildings 7 and 8 also share a single Rooftop.

When a model is on a Rooftop, it is represented by creating a stack of 10 or so large counters (such as the 5-piece Sanity or Wound markers) and placing the model on top of the stack to show that they are now high above the ground.

A model on a Rooftop is no longer in any particular space, but is rather on the entire Rooftop as a whole. All models on the same Rooftop count as being adjacent to one another, and any number of models may be on the same Rooftop.

A model on any Rooftop counts as being in the Street Area “Map Tile”.

Any model on a Rooftop counts as being in Cover from all attacks, except those from other models on the same Rooftop.

Rooftops and Line of Sight

Line of Sight to and from a model on a Rooftop is counted from the Building space closest to the target/attacker.

A model on a Rooftop always has Line of Sight to all other Rooftops on the Board, but may NOT draw Line of Sight through other Building Walls to see a target on the ground.

A model on a Rooftop does NOT have Line of Sight to or from any space within the Building below, or to any spaces directly adjacent to that Building (touching one of its Walls).

Moving to and from a Rooftop

A Hero that starts his Activation inside of a Building, may give up his movement for the turn to move up to the Rooftop. Place the Hero model on a high stack of large counters (as noted above) and move the model to be centrally located in the Building to show that they are now on the Rooftop. A Hero that is already on a Rooftop may give up their movement to move back down into the Building. Remove the stack of counters from under the model and place the model in any space of that Building (or in any space of either Building if it is a shared Rooftop).

Enemies will only move up to the Rooftop of a building if the Hero they are targeting is already up there and they need to move to be adjacent for a Melee Attack. If the Enemy ends its Move in the building, it is automatically moved to the Rooftop with its target, but loses its Attack for this Activation.

Enemies with the Shootout Standard Ability (such as Bandits) will never enter from a Hero on the same Rooftop, but instead will stay and always Melee Attack that Hero. A Hero on a Rooftop with one or more Enemies must make an Escape test to leave that Rooftop.

Bandits on Rooftops

In several of the Town Adventure Missions, some Bandits will enter play on Rooftops. These Bandits come into play with a Bandit Rifle instead of their Bandit Pistols (the details for these can be found in the Shootout in the Streets Mission). These Rooftop Bandits will never leave their Rooftop throughout the game.

All Rooftop Bandits in play should Activate and make their Shootout Attacks as a group before Activating any other Bandits that are on the ground. This helps to keep the Bandit Rifle Attacks separate from the Bandit Pistol Attacks.

Falling Off of a Rooftop

Whenever an Enemy on a Rooftop is killed by a Hero, roll a D6. On the roll of 1, 2, or 3, the Enemy falls from the Rooftop screaming and the Hero may Recover 1 Grit.

Whenever a Hero on a Rooftop takes 1 or more Wounds from an Attack, roll a D6. On the roll of 1, 2, or 3, the Hero falls from the Rooftop! Place the Hero in any ground space adjacent to the Building and the Hero takes D6 Wounds ignoring Defense.

Flying Models and Buildings

Any model that has a Flight ability may move over Buildings during its movement (ignoring Walls as it goes) to reach a target on the other side or on the Rooftop (if on the Rooftop, place the model on a stack of counters at the end of its move to show that it is now on the Rooftop as well). Unlike normal Enemies moving to Rooftops, Flying Enemies do NOT lose their Attack the turn they move to the Rooftop. To reach a target inside a Building, the model must still move through a Door to get inside.

Dynamite and Buildings

Dynamite may NOT be thrown or Bounce through Walls, except where there is a Door or Window; and then it may only be targeted at the space directly on the other side of that Door/Window (just like Line of Sight for Doors/Windows with normal Ranged Attacks). Dynamite that goes off next to a Wall WILL still hit every adjacent space, even through a Wall. Dynamite that goes off in/near a Building will not affect models on the Rooftop.

Dynamite MAY be thrown onto a Rooftop. When thrown, declare that the Rooftop is the target and place the Dynamite on any space of the Building within Range (as it is lobbed upward). As long as the Dynamite does not Bounce out of a space of that Building, it will go off, hitting every model on that Rooftop! If the Dynamite Bounces outside of a Building space, then it will land on the ground in whatever space it ends up in, ignoring models on the Rooftop and hitting models on the ground level instead.

Entry Areas

Along each of the 4 board edges there is a marked Entry Area (A, B, C, and D), highlighted in blue. Each of these Entry Areas covers 3 spaces.

Entry Areas are often used for models to enter or exit the board. When new enemies are placed at an Entry Area, they always get placed in the center space of that Entry Area first, then the two adjacent spaces of the Entry Area. It is also common for models to move onto or off of the board at an Entry Area. When this happens, simply continue the movement of that model, starting in any of the 3 Entry Area spaces. When a model moves off the board (generally only as part of a specific Mission rule), the model moves into any of the Entry Area spaces, and then moves 1 more space to move off the Board.

NEW MISSIONS

The Frontier Town Expansion has 6 new Missions to challenge your Heroes. These can be played as special Missions triggered by In-Town events or simply mixed in to your current campaign to expand your Adventures into the Town itself.

D6 Roll — Frontier Town Missions

1 — Shootout in the Streets
2 — Hanging High
3 — Bank Robbery
4 — Jail Break
5 — Town Overrun
6 — High Noon Duel (Solo)
    Random Hero plays Duel, then roll another Mission.

Playing a Mission in Town

Missions in Town work just like Missions down in the Mines; Heroes start the Adventure with 1 Grit as normal (and any Damage they might have from their previous Travel/Town Stay), one Hero takes the Old Lantern and makes Hold Back the Darkness tests, etc. To make any Town Mission more challenging, you may draw an Infamous Bandit Gang card at the start of the game and apply it to all Bandits in that Mission.

After Completing a Mission in Town

At the end of a Mission in Town, Heroes do all of the normal things for completing a Mission; rolling for Dark Stone Corruption, Fully Heal Health and Sanity; return to 1 Grit, etc.

One difference is that as long as the Heroes did not Fail Mission, they have the option to stay in the current Town instead of Traveling to a new Town. If the Heroes decide to stay in the same Town, this new Town Stay will be in the same size and Specific Town Type that the Heroes visited most recently. Everything else for the Town should still be set up fresh (Random Town Locations, etc). If the Heroes did Fail the Town Mission, they must travel to a new Town.

Reward

Failure

As the morning of his public hanging arrives, the rest of the town looks on in despair. If only someone would stand up to these dirty law-men, and bring justice back to this town!

Set Up

This Mission uses the Town Adventure Board. Place the Gallows token as well as 4 Bandits, and 10 Townsfolk markers on the board, as shown in the diagram below:

(diagram omitted but preserved as image)

In addition, if there are 3 or more Heroes in the Posse, place 2 more Bandits on Random Building Rooftops. Bandits on Rooftops have Bandit Rifles instead of Bandit Pistols, as described in the Shootout in the Streets Mission.

The Heroes may start in any of the spaces along the length of the entire Board Edge with the Entry Area marked ‘C’ on it.

Mission Goal

The Corrupt Marshals are set to hang the innocent prisoner in a public execution! You can’t let that happen! The Heroes must either kill all of the Corrupt Marshals or free the prisoner from the noose and help him to escape.

Special Rules
Corrupt Marshals

This Adventure uses the Bandit Enemies to represent the Corrupt Marshals. Corrupt Marshals use the Brutal side of the Enemy Record Sheet for Bandits (or the Brutal side plus ALL Elite Abilities if the Hero Posse is level 5 or higher), and gain an extra bonus based on the number of Heroes in the Hero Posse.

# of Heroes	Bandit Bonus
1–2	2× Health and Immune to Critical Hits
3–4	2× Health and Immune to Critical Hits, also 2× Rooftop Bandits (noted above)
5–6	3× Health and Immune to Critical Hits, also 2× Rooftop Bandits (noted above)
Alerting the Corrupt Marshals

The Corrupt Marshals are standing guard and watching the crowds. They will not Activate until the turn after they have been Alerted to the Heroes’ presence.

The Marshals will all become Alerted as soon as any Hero makes an Attack.

Also, at the end of any Hero’s Move, if they are within 3 spaces and within Line of Sight to any Corrupt Marshal (including those on the Rooftops), roll a D6. On the roll of 1 or 2, the Marshals all become Alerted.

The Gallows and Townsfolk Crowds

The Gallows marker takes up a 3×3 space area and blocks movement and Line of Sight passing through any of those spaces.

The 10 Townsfolk markers represent the crowds gathered to watch the hanging. These Townsfolk also block movement and Line of Sight from passing through their space, though they do not block Line of Sight to or from the Gallows or Rooftops. Models may move and trace Line of Sight diagonally between two Townsfolk Crowds that are touching corners. This allows Heroes to sneak up amongst the crowds to get close, without Alerting the Corrupt Marshals.

These Townsfolk Crowds will start to scatter once the shooting starts. After any model finishes making an Attack, any Townsfolk Crowd markers adjacent to it will immediately scatter (Bounce) 1 space in a Random Direction – use the Dynamite Bounce chart to do this. If the marker cannot move in that direction (because of another model or wall, etc), the Townsfolk marker simply stays where it is.

Townsfolk markers may not be attacked or targeted in any way. If a Townsfolk is in the Area of Effect of an Attack (for example, in the same or adjacent space to Dynamite or if a Hellfire marker is placed in their space), the Townsfolk marker is instantly removed and the model that made the Attack immediately gains D6 Corruption points, ignoring Willpower, for each Townsfolk removed in this way.

The Countdown

This Mission does not use the Depth Track at all. Instead, at the start of the Adventure, take the number counters marked 1–8 and set them at the top of the board, stacked face up in order, with 1 at the bottom and 8 showing at the top of the stack. This is the Countdown Stack that marks how much time is left until the lever is pulled and the Prisoner is hanged!

At the end of each turn, remove the top counter from the stack.

Also, the Heroes should still roll a Hold Back the Darkness test each turn (still getting Depth Events on doubles), needing to roll 7+ for the duration of the Adventure. Any time the Hold Back the Darkness roll is failed, remove the top counter from the Countdown Stack as well.

When the last counter is removed from the Countdown Stack, the rope drops and the Prisoner is hung! At this point, the Heroes have one last chance to save the Prisoner by shooting out the rope and setting him free!

Shooting the Rope

When the last Countdown marker is removed from the stack, the Heroes have one last chance to save the Prisoner from hanging. One Hero within Range and Line of Sight to the Gallows may take a single Shot with a Ranged Weapon outside of the normal turn sequence. Roll To Hit and for Damage as normal. The rope has no Defense, but the Hit must do 6 Wounds to successfully snap the rope.

If failed, the Prisoner is hung and the Mission is failed. If successful, the Prisoner is severed and the Prisoner is freed! Place the Escaped Prisoner marker in any empty space adjacent to the Gallows.

Prisoner on the Run

If the Prisoner is set free by the Heroes shooting out the rope, the Escaped Prisoner marker is placed on the board. At the start of each of his turn (not including the turn he was freed), the Escaped Prisoner will move D6 spaces along the shortest path toward the Entry Point marked ‘C,’ to move off the board edge and get away.

The Escaped Prisoner blocks movement, but not Line of Sight, and will not Attack, as he is running for his life. He does not need to make Escape rolls to move past Enemies.

Once in play, the Escaped Prisoner is added as a potential target for the Corrupt Marshals (when rolling for a Random Hero, include the Escaped Prisoner).

If Attacked, he has Defense 4+ and 5 Health.

Any Hero on the board may use Grit to allow the Escaped Prisoner to Re-roll his Defense rolls.

Objectives
Defeat the Corrupt Marshals

If ALL of the Corrupt Marshals are killed, or if the Prisoner is set free and is able to get away by moving off of the board at the Entry Area marked ‘C,’ the Mission is successfully completed.

Corrupt Marshal Victory

The Mission is failed if the innocent Prisoner is hung, or if he escapes the Gallows, but is killed trying to get away.

Reward

Every Hero gains 100 XP and may draw a Gear card. If all of the Corrupt Marshals were killed, also gain D6×$100.

Failure

The Heroes may not visit Town, but must begin the next Adventure directly. All Heroes gain D3 Corruption Points, ignoring Willpower, from the guilt of their failure.

Optional Rules
Using this Mission to Rescue a Hero

Sometimes a Hero in Town will be arrested (usually trying to rob the bank), and set to hang! Any time a Hero in Town would be killed by hanging, the rest of the Hero’s Posse may play this Mission to try and rescue him. During Set Up, place the captive Hero model on the center space of the Gallows token. He takes no part in the game until set free.

The Mission works exactly as written above, except that if the Rope is shot out and the Prisoner is freed, the captive Hero is immediately placed in any space adjacent to the Gallows instead of the Escaped Prisoner marker. The captive Hero does not have any of his Items or Side Bag Tokens (except those that cannot be discarded or sold, such as Parasites). Instead, he starts with 1 Grit and a simple Starting Gear Pistol tossed to him by someone in the crowd.

Starting in the turn after he is released, the captive Hero may Activate at his Initiative as normal. The Mission is failed if the captive Hero is KO’d (also, if KO’d, the captive Hero may only roll a single D6 on the Injury Chart). The Mission is won if the captive Hero moves off the board edge at the Entry Area marked ‘C,’ or if all of the Corrupt Marshals are killed.

Either way, the captive Hero regains all of his Items and Tokens at the end of the Adventure.

Money Bags

Townsfolk Hostages

Objectives
Depth Track – Defeat Enough Bank Robbers

Bank Robber Victory

Every time a Bank Robber Escapes off the board, move the Darkness 2 spaces forward on the Depth Track. If all of the Townsfolk Hostages are killed, the Bandits have gotten away with their heist and the Mission is failed.

Reward

Frontier Town – Mission 5
TOWN OVERRUN

The Town is under attack from all sides! Creatures and Demons are pouring in, sweeping through the streets and carrying townspeople off into the night! Someone needs to stand against this darkness, or this Town will be lost!

Set Up

This Mission uses the Town Adventure Board.
The 4 Entry Areas marked ‘A’, ‘B’, ‘C’, and ‘D’ are all Enemy Entry Points used by the Enemies to arrive throughout the game.

You will need several markers to use for Enemies Approaching (you can use face-down markers with the Shadows of Brimstone logo on the back for these).

Place 10 Townsfolk markers on the board, each in a Random Building.
Then place 10 more Townsfolk markers on the board, in any non-building spaces you like.
Townsfolk markers may not be placed on Building Rooftops.

Each Hero may start anywhere they choose on the board, including Building Rooftops.
Heroes start the Adventure with Grit equal to their Max Grit.

After Heroes are placed, roll a D6 for each of the 4 Enemy Entry Points.
On a roll of 1, 2, or 3, draw a Low Threat card and place those Enemies just off the board near the Entry Point.
These Enemies will move onto the board and attack at their Initiative during the first turn of the game (as detailed in the Enemy Entry Points rule below).

Mission Goal

Save the Town!
The Heroes must defeat enough Enemies to break the assault, and then kill any remaining Enemies.

Special Rules
Townsfolk

In this Mission, the Townsfolk represent all of the people of the Town running in terror from the creatures attacking!
Townsfolk markers block movement, but do not block Line of Sight for Ranged Attacks.

Each turn, immediately after the Hold Back the Darkness roll is made, Townsfolk are allowed to move around the board in an attempt to escape their fate!
All Townsfolk collectively get a single pool of Movement Points equal to whatever the Hold Back the Darkness roll was for that turn.

Heroes may divide this pool as they want between the Townsfolk markers, to move them around the board.
Townsfolk may NOT move off the board.

Townsfolk do not need to make Escape rolls to move away from Enemies and may NOT move to Rooftops.

Whenever a Random Hero is rolled for (for example, when Enemies are selecting their targets to attack), include all of the Townsfolk markers as a single “extra Hero” for that roll.

Example: An Enemy choosing a target within Move distance:
roll a D6:
1–2 = Hero A
3–4 = Hero B
5–6 = a Townsfolk marker

When a Townsfolk marker is selected like this, always use the closest Townsfolk marker to the Enemy.
As long as there are any Townsfolk markers without an Enemy in range, the Townsfolk should still be included in the Random Hero roll.

Townsfolk have 1 Health and Defense 4+.
They are immune to Sanity Damage.

Whenever a Townsfolk marker takes one or more Hits, any Hero adjacent to that Townsfolk may choose to take those Hits on themselves instead.
This must be done before Defense rolls are made for the Townsfolk.

Desperation

The Heroes are truly desperate.
Any Hero with no Enemies on their Map Tile (in the same building or in the streets) may give up their Attack to do one of the following instead:

• Scavenge
• Recover a Grit
• Ready a Once per Fight Item
• Catch Your Breath (Heal D3)

Unlimited Scavenging

During this Mission, Heroes are allowed to Scavenge as often as they want, and there is no limit to the number of times this may be done on the Town Adventure Board.

Enemy Entry Points

As noted above, the 4 Entry Areas on the board edges are Enemy Entry Points during this Mission.

At the end of each turn, roll a D6 for each Entry Point.
If a 1 or 2 is rolled (or only on a 1 if there is a Hero within 3 spaces of the Entry Area), place an Enemies Approaching marker by that Entry Point.
You can see Enemies coming, but can’t tell what they are yet!

If there is already an Enemies Approaching marker at that Entry Point, instead, remove the marker and draw a Low Threat card.
The Enemies on that card are placed just off the board near that Entry Point, as they have arrived and are ready to move onto the board (note that Enemies that have 0 Move, such as Corpse Piles, will not take part because they can’t move to enter the board).

Enemies that are not on the board yet may not be targeted or attacked in any way.
These Enemies will Activate as normal when it comes to their Initiative in the turn order and will simply move onto the board from the Entry Point (starting their move on any of the Entry Area spaces).

Enemies with the Shootout ability (such as Bandits) must move onto the board to be able to fire their Ranged Attacks (they cannot see or Attack from off the board edge).

May Not Flee

Once the Adventure begins, the Heroes may not Flee; they are trapped, and must see the Mission through to the end.

Immediate Dread

Growing Dread cards are revealed immediately when drawn, rather than being placed on the stack.

Ambushing Enemies

Whenever a group of Enemies Ambush Attacks on the Town Adventure Board, roll a Random Hero (including the Townsfolk as noted above).
Place all of those Enemies on the same Map Tile (the same building or street area) as the Hero, adjacent to Heroes as normal for an Ambush in the Mines.
If the Townsfolk were rolled as the Random Hero, the Heroes may choose which Townsfolk marker is the focus of the Ambush.
These Enemies gain +2 Initiative on their first turn as normal.

Number of Heroes

For this Mission, if you are using 1 or 2 Heroes, at the start of the game, Threat cards are only drawn for each Entry Point on the D6 roll of 1 (instead of 1, 2, or 3).

If you are using 5 or 6 Heroes, all Threat cards drawn are Med Threats instead of Low Threats.

Objectives
Depth Track – Defeat Enough Enemies

Because there is no Exploration in this Mission, the Hero Posse marker on the Depth Track is used a bit differently than normal.
Instead of marking how deep into the mine the Heroes have traveled, it marks how much of the Enemy assault the Heroes have defeated.

The Hero Posse marker starts the game on the Mine Entrance space as normal.

For every full 3 Enemies the Heroes kill, move the Hero Posse marker down 1 step on the track.
For each Large or bigger Enemy the Heroes kill, instead move the Hero Posse marker 2 steps on the track.

When the Heroes make the Hold Back the Darkness test each turn, if the roll is equal to or higher than the current position of the Hero Posse marker on the Depth Track, the assault is broken!
All Enemies Approaching markers are removed, and no more Enemies will arrive at the Entry Points.
The Heroes must now kill all remaining Enemies on the board to successfully complete the Mission (including any that might still show up through Darkness cards, etc.).

The Darkness marker on the Depth Track works as normal in this Mission.

Reward

If successful, every Hero gains:
• D6 × 10 XP
• D6 × $25 for each Townsfolk marker still alive at the end of the game
(Each Hero may choose their own reward for each of the Townsfolk markers.)

Every Hero may also draw 2 Loot cards to represent the Loot pulled from the Enemies that were defeated.

Failure

• All KO’d Heroes must roll on the Injury/Madness table as normal.
If the Darkness Escapes, any Heroes not KO’d must still roll on the Injury/Madness table.
(The Hero’s choice.)

• Also, the Town is destroyed, so the Heroes may not visit Town, but must begin the next Adventure directly.

Frontier Town Mission 6
HIGH NOON DUEL (SOLO)

The sun is high in the sky and there’s a stillness in the air. Challenged to a duel in the street by one of the locals, you face off a few paces from one another, townsfolk watching from windows and doorways, fingers twitching over holstered weapons. The church bell rings…it's time.

Set Up

This is a Solo Mission (for just a single Hero) that uses the Town Adventure Board. Place your Hero and 1 Bandit figure in the center of the street, 5 spaces apart.

(There is a small map image here.)

Mission Goal

One of the local frontier folk has challenged you to a duel in the streets at high noon! It’s one-on-one, you or him. You must out-shoot and out-fight your opponent to survive!

Special Rules
Locked Buildings

Models may not enter Buildings during this Mission.

One-on-One Duel

This Mission is for one Hero against one Enemy. There is no roll to Hold Back the Darkness and no Fleeing. The entire Mission counts as a single Fight, but there is no Loot card draw, Catching Your Breath, or Corruption Hits for Dark Stone.

Once the Adventure is complete, the Hero is restored to full Health and Sanity, returned to 1 Grit, and may continue their Town Stay or begin the next Adventure, depending on what caused the Duel.

Variable Initiative

At the start of each turn, the Hero and the Enemy each roll a D6 and add it to their Initiative for the turn.

“Draw!”

In the very first turn of the Mission, models may not Move. The Hero may still roll for Move to see if they get a Grit. This represents the initial drawing and firing of the duel.

Dueling Opponent

The Bandit model represents your opponent for the duel! He uses the stats found on the Enemy Record Sheet for the Bandit (including any Elite abilities or Brutal stats based on the Hero’s Level) and has his Health and Damage for all Attacks multiplied by the Hero’s Level (if the Hero is only Level 1, double the Bandit’s Health and Attack Damage instead).

Objectives
Defeat Your Opponent

To successfully complete the High Noon Duel (and the Mission), the Hero must defeat their opponent.

Reward

The Hero gains 25 XP × their Hero Level.

Failure

Roll for Injury as normal for being KO’d! The Hero then starts the next Adventure with no Grit.

FAQ AND CLARIFICATIONS

Q) Can a model move Diagonally around the corner of a Building Wall?
A) Yes.

Q) Can a Melee Attack be made through a Wall?
A) No. Unless there is a Door or Window that the Attacker can see his target through, directly on the other side.

Q) A model can only move straight through a Door, not diagonally through. How does this affect Diagonal Doors?
A) A model in a Diagonal Door space may not move diagonally out, around the angled corner of the Wall. Also, a model may not Lean out of a Diagonal Door (splitting the space), as the Door itself is within a single space.

Q) Does a model that is splitting a space (ie - Leaning around a Corner) block movement through both spaces? Can they Melee Attack or be Attacked from either space?
A) Yes, Yes, and Yes.

Q) If an Enemy such as Bandits have a Bounty on them, does this also count modified versions of that Enemy (ie - Infamous Bandits, or OtherWorld versions of an Enemy)?
A) Yes. A Bounty includes any Enemy that uses that Enemy Record Sheet.

Q) Do Infamous Bandits already have a Bounty on their card. Does this stack with a Bounty from the Frontier Outpost or Sheriff’s Office?
A) Yes. Just add the Bounties together for each model.

Q) The Hitchcock Gang Infamous Bandits throw Dynamite. What is the Range?
A) The Range for their Dynamite is the same as the Range for a normal Ranged Attack, 6 spaces if armed with a Bandit Pistol, 12 if armed with a Bandit Rifle. This keeps things simple and it is assumed that because the Bandit with a Rifle is on a Rooftop, he is trained to lob the Dynamite further.

Q) What happens if the Artifact cards or Devil’s Head token are accidentally moved playing The Devil’s Wheel?
A) Leave them in their new position and count the points as normal for dice partially on or fully on. Then re-set everything before the next round is played.

Q) What if a Law Hero becomes ‘Wanted’ in Town?
A) While a Hero has the ‘Wanted’ status, they gain the Keyword Outlaw. This temporarily replaces their Keyword Law until the ‘Wanted’ status is removed.

PAINTING GUIDE

This painting guide shows how to easily paint your Bandit models using only Warpaints from the Heroes of the Old West and Creatures of the Void Paint Sets.
These Shadows of Brimstone paint sets are sold separately and available now at your local game store or online at the Official Flying Frog Web Store.

STEP #1

Prime the models with matte black spray paint (use any matte - NOT Gloss- black spray paint which can be purchased inexpensively from a hardware store).

Dry brush the model with a mix of Shadow Black and Spirit White.

Base coat the pants with Portal Blue.

Base coat the boots, rope, and moneybag with Dark Flesh.

Base coat the face and hands with Light Flesh.

Base coat the mask with Saloon Red.

Base coat the shirt with a light mix of Spirit White and Shadow Black.

STEP #2

Highlight pants with a mix of Portal Blue and Spirit White.

Highlight shirt with Spirit White.

Highlight rope with Desert Yellow.

Highlight boots and moneybag with Leather Brown.

Highlight mask with a mix of Saloon Red and Spirit White.

Wash rope, boots, moneybag, and skin areas with Soft Tone Ink.

Highlight pistol, shirt buttons, holster clasp, and belt buckle with Pistol Metal.

Finish by adding mud to the bottom of the pants with Dark Flesh.
